# QSE Formation Targets briefing — bearing on the bounce vs pre-trapping fragmentation decision

**Date:** 2026-09-25
**Status:** exploratory assessment. No validated/production language.

## What the briefing establishes (plain terms)

The briefing reports a formation-number run on the QSE remnant-dark-matter framework, anchored on Trivedi & Loeb, arXiv:2509.20533v1 ("Gaussian Planck Relics are Ruled-Out as Dark Matter by LIGO", published Phys. Dark Univ. Dec 2025 — abstract verified against arXiv):

1. **LIGO rules out the Gaussian formation pathway.** Ripples in the early universe that are strong enough to collapse into enough progenitor black holes for all of dark matter (as Planck-mass relics) would also fill the sky with a gravitational-wave hum ~100x louder than LIGO's upper limit. LIGO heard nothing, so Gaussian initial fluctuations are dead as the formation route. Non-Gaussian formation is now a *required* ingredient.
2. **First abundance targets.** Initial collapse fraction β ≈ 2×10^-19 at M_prog ≈ 10^9 kg, rising to ≈ 1.5×10^-12 at M* ≈ 6×10^22 kg, each times f_sh. Fine-tuning is exactly standard-PBH tuning: a 1% shift in ripple amplitude swings the relic abundance by 50–80%.
3. **The bounce's confirmed role is narrow.** The bounce graft removes every evaporation constraint (no progenitor survives long enough to evaporate). It changes nothing about formation fine-tuning or the LIGO bound.

## Bearing on the open decision: orthogonal

The decision — **bounce vs pre-trapping fragmentation** — concerns what happens at (or just before) the quantum density limit, i.e. the *remnant-production mechanism*. The briefing's formation-number results constrain the *progenitor population at formation*, which is **upstream of that fork**. Specifically:

- The LIGO gravitational-wave bound bites on the initial ripples that collapse into progenitors. The briefing states explicitly that what happens afterward — bounce, shatter, or evaporation — changes nothing. So **neither option gains or loses from the LIGO result**.
- The abundance targets (β values) and the fine-tuning (1% → 50–80%) are identical under both options, since both start from the same progenitor population. **Neither option is favored by the formation numbers.**
- The one mechanism-specific statement in the briefing — that the bounce kills evaporation constraints — is a confirmed pro-bounce fact about *evaporation*, not formation. It does not interact with the formation-number results.

## One nuance worth recording

If the bounce option was partly attractive because it might ease the formation fine-tuning, the briefing closes that off: "The bounce graft changes nothing about fine-tuning." That removes a possible (mistaken) pro-bounce argument, but it does **not** constitute an argument for fragmentation — it leaves the two options exactly where they were relative to each other.

## What the briefing actually elevates

The briefing identifies **non-Gaussian formation** as the single most load-bearing missing piece of the whole framework — and that problem sits upstream of the bounce/fragmentation fork. In other words, the open decision is now *second-order* relative to the formation problem: whichever remnant-production mechanism Jase picks, the framework still needs a formation scenario that threads the LIGO bound. Resolving the formation question first may end up constraining the mechanism choice anyway (e.g., a formation scenario that delivers progenitors in a particular density regime could favor one option).

## Bottom line for Jase

The briefing does not pick a side. Its results apply equally to both options, so **the bounce-vs-fragmentation decision cannot be settled from the formation numbers** — it must still be made on other grounds (which mechanism is physically better motivated, which the shatter criterion actually describes, etc.). What the briefing does do is (a) remove "helps formation fine-tuning" from the pro-bounce column, and (b) tell you the more urgent problem is upstream: non-Gaussian progenitor formation.
